The idea

A driving decision is also
a decision about other people.

Accommodating passenger preferences while responding to uncertain human driving behavior remains a central challenge. Interaction-driven games model how drivers respond to one another; intention-driven language reasoning interprets passenger requests and observed behavior. LLMGame connects the two through driving-style parameters and probabilistic beliefs over surrounding drivers’ behavioral types.

01 / Method

Two timescales.
One coordinated decision.

Slow reasoning supplies context.
Fast optimization turns it into motion.

01

Understand the interaction

Select relevant surrounding vehicles and represent the local driving context together with passenger instructions.

02

Reason about intent

The slow system updates probabilities over Yield, Normal, and Force types and maps passenger preferences to bounded driving-style parameters and safety, efficiency, and comfort weights.

03

Choose and execute

The ego vehicle acts as a Stackelberg leader and anticipates type-dependent follower responses. The fast system selects lateral maneuvers and longitudinal acceleration under safety and motion constraints.

Inside the slow system

From human intention
to decision parameters.

Scene information, recent motion histories, road context, and the latest passenger instruction form the reasoning input. A structured prompt combines these observations with parameter definitions and driving knowledge.

The LLM returns driving-style parameters and behavioral-type probabilities. These outputs condition the fast system’s evaluation of candidate maneuvers and surrounding-vehicle responses.

Reasoning runs asynchronously, allowing planning and control to continue between updates. Passenger preferences shape the desired behavior; inferred type beliefs shape the anticipated interaction.

04 / Real-vehicle experiments

Taking the interaction
onto a real road.

Tongji Smart Town test site · Shanghai
Interaction with a human-driven vehicle

The trials take place on a two-way, two-lane road in the western area of the test site. The black Hongqi autonomous vehicle (AV) must merge from the inner lane into the outer lane before an intersection, while the silver BYD human-driven vehicle (HV) responds to its merging maneuver.

These sequences illustrate intention communication through motion and recognition of human responses: observed HV behavior informs the slow system’s inference, and the fast system translates the updated beliefs into speed and lane-change decisions. Both recordings are provided in full. These qualitative field examples are separate from the controlled simulation ablation.
